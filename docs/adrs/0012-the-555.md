# 12. The 555 carries one bit between steps, and shows six of its eight pins

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-24
- **Ticket:** C-60
- **Deciders:** Denis Bourge

## Context and problem statement

ADR 0010 left the 555 out for two reasons: it has internal state, a flip-flop,
which no element had carried; and it only earns its place beside a signal that
changes. ADR 0011 settled the second — and a 555 *is* the signal source a
hobbyist actually builds, from a battery, two resistors and a capacitor.

What is left is how an element carries a bit, and which of its pins it shows.

## Decision

**A behavioural 555 on the terms of ADR 0010**, with two particulars.

1. **The flip-flop is carried state, like a capacitor's charge.** Within a
   solve it is fixed, so the device the solver sees is smooth: an output
   driven high or low (the saturating source of C-53, with no gain), a
   discharge switch on or off, and the internal divider that sets the
   thresholds at a third and two thirds of the supply. After every step the
   thresholds are read from the solved point and the bit is updated: the
   trigger below a third sets it, the threshold above two thirds resets it,
   and the trigger wins if both are true, as the datasheet has it. The bit
   lives in the same map the stepper already carries a capacitor's voltage in,
   so it survives an edit the way charge does (C-48).

2. **Six pins: VCC, GND, TRIG, THR, DIS, OUT.** RESET is taken as tied to VCC
   and CONTROL as left alone, which is how nearly every beginner circuit wires
   them. Eight pins would need every one wired before the part could be
   stamped at all, and an unconnected CONTROL pin is correct on a breadboard.
   The part is still one real part: a NE555 in DIP-8, and when the breadboard
   view (B-14) and the KiCad export (B-17) reach it, they map the six onto its
   eight with pin 4 joined to pin 8 and pin 5 free.

What it honours: the two thresholds; the latch, with the trigger dominant; an
output that swings to within the datasheet's volt and a bit of VCC and a tenth
of a volt of GND, with its current limit; a discharge transistor that sinks.
What it ignores: RESET and CONTROL; the output's rise and fall times; the
exact supply current beyond the divider's.

## Consequences

1. **The bit changes once per step, not mid-step.** A step that carries a
   threshold crossing has its whole-step and half-step answers disagree —
   one sees the flip, the other does not — so the error estimate rejects it
   and the stepper closes in on the crossing. An astable's period comes out
   within a few per cent of 1.44 / ((R1 + 2·R2)·C), and a test holds it to that.
2. **`MAX_TERMINALS` goes to six.** Every coupled device's arrays grow by one
   row and column; the frame budget of C-15 is what says whether that is free.
3. **A circuit with a 555 runs on the stepper** even with nothing reactive in
   it, as a circuit with a signal generator does (ADR 0011).
